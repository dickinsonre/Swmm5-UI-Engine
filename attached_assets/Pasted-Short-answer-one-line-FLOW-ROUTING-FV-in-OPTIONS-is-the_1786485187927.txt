Short answer: one line. FLOW_ROUTING FV in [OPTIONS] is the entire requirement — all twenty-one FV_ keys have defaults and the solver reads everything else from the model you already have.

But four of them decide whether the run is any good.

The ones that actually matter
Key	Default	Why you'd touch it
FV_CELL_LENGTH	0	The one to set. 0 means no Δx target, so every conduit gets exactly FV_MIN_CELLS. Set a real Δx whenever peak flows or in-conduit profiles matter.
FV_MIN_CELLS	4	A floor, applied with or without a Δx target. At 4 cells the manual measures 15.3 % mean peak attenuation on Example1 against 37.1 % at one cell and 7.6 % at eight — cause is geometric, not diffusive, so FV_ORDER 2 won't rescue an unresolved mesh.
FV_SLOT_CELERITY	100	Sets the slot width through T_slot = g·A_full/c², capped at 5 % of the section width. This is a direct accuracy/cost dial: a physical acoustic celerity of 1000+ ft/s would collapse your step, since Δt is bounded by Δx/(|v|+c).
FV_CFL	0.5	Courant number. Rarely needs moving.

FV_CELL_LENGTH and FV_SLOT_CELERITY are in project display units — the parser converts to internal feet, the same way HEAD_TOLERANCE is handled.

The rest, grouped

Scheme: FV_ORDER (1), FV_LIMITER (MINMOD), FV_TIME_INTEGRATION (EULER), FV_RIEMANN (HLLC). Order 2 is safe to leave on — it reconstructs (η, v) rather than (h, Q), so a flat surface has exactly zero slope and it degenerates to first order at rest. On Ritter I measured it nearly halving the error (3.30 % → 1.77 %) at no cost to still water. FV_RIEMANN only affects transport; hydraulics are bit-identical either way.

Node coupling: FV_NODE_COUPLING (SEMI_IMPLICIT), FV_NODE_PICARD (1), FV_NODE_CELL_COUPLING (NO), FV_NODE_DT (STABILITY). Leave these alone unless you're chasing performance — and note the manual argues against doing so: FV_NODE_DT NONE is 24–134× faster and degrades L1 depth error by 3 to 49× on the frictional and transcritical cases.

Performance: FV_LTS (YES), FV_LTS_MAX_TIERS (6), FV_COMPACTION (YES), FV_CFL_CENSUS_INTERVAL (1), FV_BACKEND (AUTO), FV_MIN_PARALLEL_CELLS (20000), FV_STRUCTURE_COUPLING (SUBSTEP).

Transport: FV_SCALAR_SCHEME (MUSCL), FV_DISPERSION (0 — accepted but inert, and warns at open, until FV transport is connected).

What the model itself has to supply

This is the part that bites, and none of it is an option key:

Every conduit needs real cross-section geometry. A conduit whose full depth or area is zero is rejected at initialization rather than silently routed.
A conduit loop closed entirely by virtual junctions cannot be meshed — such a cycle has no boundary. An ordinary junction breaks it.
Hot starts aren't interchangeable between routing models. A dynamic-wave hot start projects onto the mesh with a warning; the reverse discards in-conduit structure.
What it inherits from your existing parameters

LENGTHENING_STEP acts as a Δx floor exactly as it acts as a length floor for dynamic wave — the solver meshes the Courant-lengthened mod_length and uses the same adjusted slope and roughness. MIN_SURFAREA becomes the junction storage area, which is why it matters more here than you'd expect: as an effective length A_s/T it's a few feet against a conduit Δx of several hundred, so the manhole, not the pipe, sets the explicit substep. Manning's n, entrance/exit losses, SURCHARGE_DEPTH, ponded area, storage curves, outfall types, culvert codes in [XSECTIONS], flap gates, force-main friction and [VIRTUAL_JUNCTIONS] are all read and honoured. ROUTING_STEP becomes a reporting and forcing cadence rather than a stability constraint — the solver substeps internally to fill it.

One portability warning: the keys are inert rather than rejected under KINWAVE/STEADY/DYNWAVE, so switching FLOW_ROUTING never invalidates the file within OpenSWMM. Handing that same file to stock EPA SWMM 5.2.4 is a different matter — I've verified 5.2.4 dies with a fatal ERROR 205 on the SWMM6 [OPTIONS] keywords, and I'd expect the same on FV_*, though I haven't run that specific case.

The Options tab in the app builds an [OPTIONS] block from any subset of these and exports it as a .inp snippet, if it's easier to tick than to type.
